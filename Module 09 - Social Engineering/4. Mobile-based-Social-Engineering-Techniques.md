# 🛡️ 4. Mobile-based Social Engineering Techniques
### CEH v13 · Module 09: Social Engineering

> **ℹ️ About these Notes**
> Mobile devices are high-value targets for social engineers because they contain personal, financial, and authentication data (SMS 2FA) in one place. This document explores how attackers use malicious apps, SMS lures, and QR codes to compromise mobile security.

## 1. Malicious & Repackaged Apps
Attackers exploit the popularity of mobile gaming and utility apps to distribute malware.

*   **Publishing Malicious Apps:** Creating an app with attractive features (e.g., a "free" trending game) and publishing it on app stores under a popular name. Once installed, it steals credentials and contacts.
*   **Repackaging Legitimate Apps:** 
    1.  Attacker downloads a popular, legitimate game from a store.
    2.  The app is **repackaged** with a malicious payload (Trojan/Spyware).
    3.  The infected version is uploaded to a **third-party app store**.
    4.  Unsuspecting users download it, granting the attacker full access to their device data.

## 2. Fake Security Applications
This is a sophisticated "Multi-Device" attack used to bypass 2FA (Two-Factor Authentication).
1.  **PC Infection:** The attacker first infects the victim's computer.
2.  **The Trigger:** When the victim logs into their bank, a fake pop-up appears on the PC claiming they need a "security app" on their phone to receive authentication codes.
3.  **The Hook:** The victim downloads the app (malware) onto their phone.
4.  **The Theft:** The mobile malware intercepts the **SMS-based 2FA code** sent by the bank and forwards it to the attacker, allowing them to drain the account.

---

## 3. SMiShing (SMS Phishing)
SMiShing uses the SMS system to provoke **instant action** through fear or greed.
*   **The Fear Lure:** "Urgent: Your XIM Bank account has been locked. Call 1-800-XXX-XXXX to verify your identity."
*   **The Greed Lure:** "Congratulations! You have been selected as a lucky winner of $1,000. Click here to claim: [malicious-link]."
*   **The Trap:** Victims either call a fraudulent number (where a recording asks for credit card/PIN data) or click a link that installs mobile malware.

---

## 4. QRLJacking (QR Code Hijacking)
QRLJacking exploits the **QR Code Login** method (Quick Response Code Login) used by many web applications to hijack sessions.

### How QRLJacking Works:
1.  **Cloning:** The attacker clones a legitimate login QR code from a target site (like WhatsApp or a banking portal).
2.  **Phishing Page:** The attacker places the cloned QR code on a fake, well-crafted phishing page.
3.  **Refreshing:** A script keeps the QR code "fresh" so it doesn't expire.
4.  **Scanning:** The victim scans the malicious QR code with their mobile app, thinking they are logging into a real service.
5.  **Hijacking:** The victim's session tokens are sent to the attacker's server, granting them full account access and device info (GPS, IMEI, SIM details).

---

## 5. QR Code Cloning Tools
*   **QR TIGER:** A generator that can clone static and dynamic QR codes. It allows attackers to track the number of scans, location, and device type used by the victim. *(https://www.qrcode-tiger.com)*
*   **Other Tools:** *QR Code KIT, Soti MobiControl, QR Code Generator.*

---

### 📝 Module Summary
Mobile social engineering bridges the gap between digital and physical attacks. By leveraging the trust users place in their phones and the convenience of QR codes, attackers can bypass even robust 2FA systems. Users should avoid third-party app stores and be highly skeptical of unsolicited SMS messages or QR codes in public places.
