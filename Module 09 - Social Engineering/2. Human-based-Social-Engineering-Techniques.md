# 🛡️ 2. Human-based Social Engineering Techniques
### CEH v13 · Module 09: Social Engineering

> **ℹ️ About these Notes**
> This document explores techniques that rely on direct human interaction. Attackers exploit social norms—such as helpfulness, trust, and fear of authority—to bypass security barriers. Understanding these tactics is critical for both physical security and employee awareness training.

## 1. Impersonation (Physical & Phone)
The attacker pretends to be a legitimate person (courier, janitor, technician) to gain unauthorized access.

### Common Impersonation Roles & Examples:
*   **Legitimate End-User:** Pretending to be an employee who "forgot" a password. 
    *   *Example:* "Hi! This is John from finance. I've forgotten my password. Can I get it?"
*   **Important User (VIP):** Posing as a VP or Director to intimidate.
    *   *Example:* "This is Kevin, the CFO’s Secretary. I’m working on an urgent project and need my password reset NOW."
*   **Technical Support:** Posing as a vendor/technician to "fix" a nonexistent problem.
    *   *Example:* "This is Mathew from tech support. We had a system crash; I need your ID and password to check for lost data."
*   **Internal Employee/Vendor:** Dressing in business clothes or a uniform to roam the office.
*   **Repairman:** Electricians or HVAC repairpersons planting snooping devices.
    *   *Example:* "I'm with Aircon Express. I heard the computer room is too warm; I need to check the HVAC."

> **💡 CEH Beginner Tip:** Impersonation isn't just about the "acting"—it's about the **costume**. A simple high-visibility vest and a clipboard can grant an attacker access to many restricted areas without a single question.

## 2. Vishing (Voice Phishing)
Vishing uses VoIP technology and **Caller ID Spoofing** to trick victims into revealing financial or personal data.

### Vishing Tactics:
*   **Abusing Helpfulness:** Targeting help-desk staff who reset passwords out of pity.
    *   *Example:* Claiming you'll be fired if you miss a deadline to bypass verification.
*   **Third-Party Authorization:** Claiming to be authorized by an official who is currently on vacation.
    *   *Example:* "I spoke with Mr. XYZ before he left; he said you could provide this info in his absence."
*   **Fake Troubleshooting:** Calling to "check service" due to a reported network slowdown.
*   **Trusted Authority Figure:** Pretending to be a fire marshal, auditor, or superintendent.
    *   *Example:* "I'm John Brown with the external auditor. I need 10 minutes to inspect your disaster recovery."

## 3. Eavesdropping & Shoulder Surfing
*   **Eavesdropping:** Intercepting audio, video, or written communication (emails, IMs) to overhear passwords or business plans.
*   **Shoulder Surfing:** Observing a victim entering credentials. Attackers may use binoculars or hidden cameras for long-distance surfing.

---

## 4. Dumpster Diving
Rummaging through trash to find sensitive data. Common finds include:
*   **Phone Lists:** Employee names and contact numbers.
*   **Organizational Charts:** Details on infrastructure and restricted areas.
*   **Memos & Faxes:** Personal details and internal instructions.
*   **Policy Manuals:** Information on system operations.
*   **Calendars/Logs:** Reveal the best times to plan a physical or network attack.

---

## 5. Reverse Social Engineering
The attacker assumes the role of a "knowledgeable professional" so that **victims seek them out for help**.
1.  **Sabotage:** The attacker creates a problem (e.g., corrupting a workstation).
2.  **Marketing:** The attacker advertises their "help" (leaving a business card or adding their number to a fake error message).
3.  **Support:** The victim calls the attacker, who "fixes" the problem while extracting sensitive data.

---

## 6. Piggybacking vs. Tailgating
These terms are often confused but have a key difference regarding **consent**.

| Technique | Consent | Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Piggybacking** | **With Consent** | An attacker asks an authorized person to hold the door (e.g., "I forgot my badge"). |
| **Tailgating** | **Without Consent** | An attacker closely follows an authorized person through a door before it closes. |

---

## 7. Advanced Deception Techniques
*   **Diversion Theft:** Tricking delivery drivers into moving consignments to the wrong location ("Round the Corner Game").
*   **Honey Trap:** Creating a fake online relationship to extract data from an insider.
*   **Baiting:** Leaving a malware-infected USB drive (labeled "Salary Info") in a public area like an elevator.
*   **Quid Pro Quo:** Offering a service (like "free tech support") in exchange for credentials.
*   **Elicitation:** Using disarming, normal conversation to extract specific info.
*   **Bait and Switch:** Offering a product at a low price via a link that actually installs malware or steals data.

---

### 📝 Module Summary
Human-based social engineering succeeds by weaponizing common courtesy and social hierarchy. Whether through the pressure of a fake "VIP" call or the simple act of holding a door, these techniques prove that a single psychological slip can compromise an entire organization's technical defenses.
