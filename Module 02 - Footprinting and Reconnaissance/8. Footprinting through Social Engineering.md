## 1. What is Social Engineering?

Social engineering is a non-technical process where an attacker misleads a person into inadvertently providing confidential information. 
- The target is unaware that someone is stealing their data.
- The attacker exploits human gullibility and willingness to help.
- **Process:** Gain confidence of an authorized user, then mislead them into revealing sensitive info.
- **Goals:** Identity theft, industrial espionage, network intrusion, and fraud.
- **Information Gathered:** Credit card details, SSNs, usernames/passwords, IP addresses, server names, and network layouts.
- **Methods:** Eavesdropping, shoulder surfing, dumpster diving, impersonation, tailgating, piggybacking, reverse social engineering.

## 2. Collecting Information on Social Networking Sites

Social networking sites (LinkedIn, Facebook, Instagram, Twitter, Pinterest, YouTube) are rapidly growing platforms designed to connect friends or build professional networks.
- They are open to everyone, making them valuable sources for attackers.
- Attackers browse public profiles or create fake profiles (posing as genuine users) to gather info (DOB, education, employment, upcoming news).
- By sending fake friend requests, attackers can gain access to restricted, private pages of the victim if the request is accepted.

## 3. Information Available on Social Networking Sites

Users often share extensive personal information, chat with friends, post photos/videos, join groups, and create events. If privacy settings are misconfigured, attackers gain full access to this data.

**What Individuals Reveal:**
| What Users Do | What Attacker Gets |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Maintain profile** | Contact info, location, and related information |
| **Connect to friends, chat** | Friends list, friends’ info, and related information |
| **Share photos and videos** | Identity of family members, interests, and related information |
| **Play games, join groups** | Interests |
| **Create events** | Activities |

Organizations also use these platforms to promote products and gather feedback, which can be exploited.

**What Organizations Reveal:**
| What Organizations Do | What Attacker Gets |
| :--- | :--- |
| **User surveys** | Business strategies |
| **Promote products** | Product profile |
| **User support** | Social engineering targets |
| **Recruitment** | Platform/technology information |
| **Background check to hire employees** | Type of business |

## 4. Eavesdropping, Shoulder Surfing, Dumpster Diving, and Impersonation

These are physical and auditory social engineering techniques used to collect information.

### Eavesdropping
The act of intercepting communication (audio, video, text) without consent.
- Includes tapping phone conversations, intercepting faxes, or reading instant messaging.

### Shoulder Surfing
Secretly observing the target to gain critical information.
- The attacker stands behind the victim to watch keystrokes (passwords, PINs, credit card numbers).
- Highly effective in crowded places.

### Dumpster Diving (Trashing)
An uncouth technique involving rummaging through garbage bins.
- **Targets:** Target company's trash bins, printer waste bins, sticky notes at desks, ATM trash bins.
- **Information Gathered:** Phone bills, financial info, printouts of source codes, account info, and sensitive company documents.

### Impersonation
The attacker pretends to be a legitimate or authorized person.
- **Roles:** Courier/delivery person, janitor, businessman, client, technician, or visitor.
- **Action:** Can be done in person, over the phone, or via email. The attacker gains access to search desks, rummage bins, overhear conversations, or shoulder surf while posing as an authorized individual.
