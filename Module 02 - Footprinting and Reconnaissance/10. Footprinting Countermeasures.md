## 1. Defensive Countermeasures

To protect against footprinting, organizations must enforce policies and configure systems to offset information disclosure. 

**Policy and Training Measures:**
- Restrict employees’ access to social networking sites from the organization’s network.
- Educate employees to use pseudonyms on blogs, groups, and forums.
- Develop and enforce security policies (information security and password policies) to regulate what information employees can reveal to third parties.
- Conduct periodic security awareness training to educate employees on social engineering tricks and risks.
- Train employees to thwart social engineering techniques and attacks.
- Ensure no critical information (strategic plans, product info, sales projections) is displayed on notice boards or walls.

**Information Disclosure Measures:**
- Do not reveal critical information in press releases, annual reports, or product catalogs.
- Limit the amount of information published on a website or the Internet.
- Use footprinting techniques against your own organization to discover and remove any publicly available sensitive info.
- Sanitize the details provided to Internet registrars to hide direct contact details.
- Place critical documents (business plans, proprietary documents) offline.
- Keep the domain name profile private and opt for privacy services on a Whois lookup database.
- Request `archive.org` to delete the history of the website from the archive database.

**Technical Configuration Measures:**
- Configure web servers to avoid information leakage.
- Disable directory listings in web servers.
- Configure Internet Information Services (IIS) to avoid information disclosure through banner grabbing.
- Hide servers behind a secure proxy.
- Set apart internal and external DNS, or use split DNS, and restrict zone transfer to authorized servers.
- Prevent search engines from caching a web page and use anonymous registration services.
- Implement multi-factor authentication (MFA) mechanisms.
- Avoid domain-level cross-linking for critical assets.
- Encrypt and password-protect sensitive information.
- Implement captchas and rate limiting on public-facing services to prevent automated tools from collecting data rapidly.
- Do not enable protocols that are not required.
- Always use TCP/IP and IPsec filters for defense in depth.
- Disable the geo-tagging functionality on cameras to prevent geolocation tracking, and turn off geolocation access on mobile devices when not needed.
- Disable or delete the accounts of employees who have left the organization.
- Configure mail servers to ignore emails from anonymous individuals.
- Deploy honeypots or honeynets within the network to attract, detect, and divert potential footprinters away from critical systems.
