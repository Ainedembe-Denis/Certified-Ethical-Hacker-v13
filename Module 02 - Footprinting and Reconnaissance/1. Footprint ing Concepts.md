## 1. Reconnaissance / Footprinting

Reconnaissance (or footprinting) is the preparatory phase where an attacker seeks to gather maximum information about a target evaluation before launching an attack. 

- It is the first step in ethical hacking, referring to collecting info about a target network and environment.
- It identifies the level of risk associated with publicly accessible information.
- Completed systematically, it produces a **blueprint** (unique system profile) of the target organization.
- There is no single methodology for footprinting, but the organized activity helps uncover existing vulnerabilities and identifying ways to exploit them.

---

## 2. Types of Footprinting/Reconnaissance

Footprinting is categorized into passive and active methods.

### Passive Footprinting
Gathering information about the target without direct interaction. It is mainly useful when activities must remain undetected. It is technically difficult because no active traffic is sent to the target organization; only archived/stored data is collected.
- **Involves:**
  - Open-source Intelligence (OSINT) gathering
  - Proprietary databases and paid services
  - Sharing intelligence with partner organizations or industry groups

### Active Footprinting
Gathering information about the target with direct interaction. The target may recognize the ongoing information gathering process. Active footprinting requires more preparation as it leaves traces that may alert the organization.
- **Involves:**
  - DNS interrogation
  - Social engineering
  - Network/port scanning
  - User and service enumeration

## 3. Information Obtained in Footprinting

Conducting footprinting across different network levels yields specific categories of data, helping attackers gain access to sensitive data or mount attacks.

### Organization Information
Information available from the target's website and Whois databases.
- Employee details (names, contact addresses, designations, work experience)
- Addresses and mobile/telephone numbers
- Branch and location details
- Partners of the organization
- Web links to other company-related sites
- Background of the organization and web technologies
- News articles, press releases, legal documents, patents, and trademarks

*Attackers use this to identify key personnel and launch social engineering attacks.*

### Network Information
Gathered by performing Whois database analysis and trace routing.
- Domain and sub-domains
- Network blocks and topology
- Trusted routers and firewalls
- IP addresses of reachable systems
- Whois records, DNS records, and related information

### System Information
Gathered by performing network, DNS, website, and email footprinting.
- Web server OS and locations
- Publicly available email addresses
- Usernames, passwords, etc.

## 4. Objectives of Footprinting

- **Identify the easiest entry point:** Finding the simplest way to break through the organization's security perimeter.
- **Analyze security posture:** Determine the placement of firewalls, proxies, and other security solutions to identify loopholes.
- **Reduce the target scope:** Take an unknown entity and reduce it to specific domains, network blocks, and individual IP addresses of Internet-connected systems.
- **Identify the weakest link:** Build a database of security weaknesses to find the most vulnerable points in the perimeter.

---

## 5. Footprinting Threats

Footprinting exposes an organization to various threats:
- **Social Engineering:** Gathering crucial information directly or indirectly from willing employees unaware of the attacker's intent, without using intrusion methods.
- **System and Network Attacks:** Using info on system configurations and OS types to find and exploit vulnerabilities, taking control of systems or the entire network.
- **Information Leakage:** If sensitive info falls into the hands of attackers, they can mount an attack or use it for monetary benefit.
- **Privacy Loss:** Accessing systems and escalating privileges to admin levels results in privacy loss for the organization and its personnel.
- **Corporate Espionage:** Competitors acquiring sensitive data to launch similar products, alter prices, and undermine the market position.
- **Business Loss:** Malicious attacks result in billions of dollars lost for e-commerce, banking, and finance businesses.

---

## 6. Footprinting Methodology

The footprinting methodology is a procedure for collecting information about a target organization from all available sources. 
It involves gathering data like URLs, locations, establishment details, number of employees, domain ranges, and contact info. Attackers collect this from publicly accessible sources such as search engines, social networking sites, and Whois databases.
