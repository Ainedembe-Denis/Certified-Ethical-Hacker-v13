## 1. What is Hacking?

Hacking in the field of computer security refers to exploiting system vulnerabilities and compromising security controls to gain unauthorized or inappropriate access to system resources. It involves modifying system or application features to achieve a goal outside its creator’s original purpose. 

Hacking can be done to steal, pilfer, or redistribute intellectual property, leading to business loss. It is generally done using scripts or other network programming.

**Network hacking techniques include:**
- Creating viruses and worms
- Performing denial-of-service (DoS) attacks
- Establishing unauthorized remote access connections (trojans/backdoors)
- Creating botnets
- Packet sniffing
- Phishing
- Password cracking

**Motives behind hacking:** Stealing critical information, thrill, intellectual challenge, curiosity, experiment, knowledge, financial gain, prestige, power, peer recognition, vengeance, and vindictiveness.

## 2. Who is a Hacker?

A hacker is an intelligent individual with excellent computer skills, along with the ability to create and explore the computer’s software and hardware. They are a person who breaks into a system or network without authorization to destroy, steal sensitive data, or perform malicious attacks.

Usually, a hacker is a skilled engineer or programmer with enough knowledge to discover vulnerabilities in a target system. They enjoy learning the details of programming languages and computer systems.

For some, hacking is a hobby (gaining knowledge), while others have malicious intent behind their escapades (stealing business data, credit cards, SSNs, and passwords).

## 3. Hackers and their Motivations

Hackers fall into various categories based on their activities and intentions:

- **Script Kiddies:** Unskilled hackers who compromise systems by running scripts and tools developed by real hackers. Focus on quantity over quality, aiming for popularity.
- **White Hat Hackers:** Penetration testers or security analysts who use their skills for defensive purposes with permission from the system owner.
- **Black Hat Hackers:** Individuals (also known as crackers) who use their extraordinary computing skills for illegal or malicious purposes.
- **Gray Hat Hackers:** Work both offensively and defensively at various times, sometimes finding vulnerabilities to help vendors improve products.
- **Hacktivists:** Hackers who break into government or corporate systems as an act of protest to promote political/social agendas or deface websites.
- **State-Sponsored Hackers:** Highly skilled individuals employed by a government to penetrate and gain top-secret intelligence from other governments or military organizations.
- **Cyber Terrorists:** Motivated by religious or political beliefs to create fear through large-scale disruption of computer networks.
- **Corporate Spies (Industrial Spies):** Perform corporate espionage by stealing critical information (blueprints, formulas, trade secrets) from competitors, often using APTs.
- **Blue Hat Hackers:** Contract-based cybersecurity professionals hired by organizations to evaluate systems before malicious actors exploit them.
- **Red Hat Hackers:** Adopt aggressive, proactive tactics like black hats to neutralize threats, often going beyond mere defense to take down black hat hackers directly.
- **Green Hat Hackers:** Newcomers motivated by the desire to become skilled professionals, spending time studying and practicing ethical hacking.
- **Suicide Hackers:** Individuals aiming to bring down critical infrastructure for a “cause” without concern for jail terms or consequences (similar to suicide bombers).
- **Hacker Teams:** A consortium of skilled hackers with their own resources and funding, synergizing to develop advanced tools and execute planned attacks.
- **Insiders:** Trusted employees with privileged access who violate rules or intentionally cause harm, usually disgruntled or undertrained.
- **Criminal Syndicates:** Organized groups involved in planned, prolonged criminal activities across distinct jurisdictions, aiming to embezzle money and launder it.
- **Organized Hackers:** Highly organized hierarchical groups of hardened criminals using rented botnets and crimeware services for massive cyber-attacks.
