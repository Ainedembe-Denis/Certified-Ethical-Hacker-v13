## 1. Elements of Information Security

Information security is “the state of the well-being of information and infrastructure in which the possibility of theft, tampering, or disruption of information and services is kept low or tolerable.” It relies on five major elements:

- **Confidentiality:** Assurance that information is accessible only to authorized users. Controls include data classification, data encryption, and proper disposal of equipment.
- **Integrity:** The trustworthiness of data or resources in the prevention of improper and unauthorized changes. Measures include checksums and access control.
- **Availability:** Assurance that the systems responsible for delivering, storing, and processing information are accessible when required by authorized users. Measures include disk arrays, clustered machines, antivirus software, and DDoS prevention systems.
- **Authenticity:** Characteristic of communication, documents, or data that ensures the quality of being genuine or uncorrupted. Controls include biometrics, smart cards, and digital certificates.
- **Non-Repudiation:** A way to guarantee that the sender of a message cannot later deny having sent it and the recipient cannot deny having received it. Utilizes digital signatures.

## 2. Information Security Attacks: Motives, Goals, and Objectives

An attack is an action performed with the intent to breach an IT system’s security by exploiting its vulnerabilities.

> **Formula:** Attacks = Motive (Goal) + Method (TTP) + Vulnerability

**Motives behind information security attacks:**
- Disrupt business continuity
- Perform information theft
- Manipulating data
- Create fear and chaos by disrupting critical infrastructures
- Bring financial loss to the target
- Propagate religious or political beliefs
- Achieve a state’s military objectives
- Damage the reputation of the target
- Take revenge
- Demand ransom

## 3. Tactics, Techniques, and Procedures (TTPs)

TTPs refer to the patterns of activities and methods associated with specific threat actors or groups.
- **Tactics:** A strategy followed by an attacker to perform the attack from beginning to end. Helps to predict and detect evolving threats early.
- **Techniques:** Technical methods used by an attacker to achieve intermediate results during the attack. Helps to identify vulnerabilities and implement defensive measures.
- **Procedures:** A systematic approach followed by threat actors to launch an attack. Helps identify what the attacker is looking for within the infrastructure.

## 4. Vulnerabilities

A vulnerability is a weakness in the design or implementation of a system that can be exploited to compromise its security. 

**Common Reasons for Vulnerabilities:**
- **Hardware or software misconfiguration:** E.g., unencrypted protocols or default settings.
- **Insecure or poor design of network and application:** E.g., improperly implemented firewalls or VPNs.
- **Inherent technology weaknesses:** E.g., systems running old browser versions prone to DoS.
- **End-user carelessness:** E.g., falling for social engineering or sharing credentials.
- **Intentional end-user acts:** E.g., disgruntled ex-employees revealing sensitive data.

### Examples of Vulnerabilities

**Technological Vulnerabilities:**
| Vulnerability | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **TCP/IP protocol** | HTTP, FTP, ICMP, SNMP, SMTP are inherently insecure |
| **Operating System** | Inherently insecure or not patched with the latest updates |
| **Network Device** | Routers, firewalls, and switches vulnerable due to lack of passwords, authentication, insecure routing, or firewall flaws |

**Configuration Vulnerabilities:**
| Vulnerability | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **User account** | Insecure transmission of user account details (usernames, passwords) |
| **System account** | Setting weak passwords for system accounts |
| **Internet service misconfig** | Misconfiguring IIS, Apache, FTP, and Terminal services (e.g., enabling dangerous JavaScript) |
| **Default settings** | Leaving network devices with default passwords and settings |
| **Network device misconfig** | Misconfiguring network devices |

## 5. Classification of Attacks

According to IATF, security attacks are classified into five categories:

1. **Passive Attacks:** Intercepting and monitoring network traffic without tampering. Difficult to detect.
   - *Examples:* Footprinting, sniffing, network traffic analysis, decryption of weakly encrypted traffic.
2. **Active Attacks:** Tampering with data in transit or disrupting communication to break into secured systems.
   - *Examples:* DoS, bypassing protection, malware (viruses, ransomware), spoofing, replay, password attacks, session hijacking, MitM, DNS/ARP poisoning.
3. **Close-in Attacks:** Performed when the attacker is in close physical proximity with the target.
   - *Examples:* Social engineering (eavesdropping, shoulder surfing, dumpster diving).
4. **Insider Attacks:** Performed by trusted persons who have physical access to critical assets.
   - *Examples:* Eavesdropping, wiretapping, theft of physical devices, data theft and spoliation, pod slurping, planting keyloggers/backdoors.
5. **Distribution Attacks:** Attackers tamper with hardware or software prior to installation (at source or in transit).
   - *Examples:* Modification of software/hardware during production or distribution.

## 6. Information Warfare

Information warfare (InfoWar) is the use of information and communication technologies (ICT) for competitive advantages over an opponent.
*Source:* [https://iwar.org.uk](https://iwar.org.uk)

**Categories of Information Warfare (Martin Libicki):**
- **Command and Control (C2) warfare:** Impact an attacker possesses over a compromised system they control.
- **Intelligence-based warfare:** Sensor-based technology that corrupts technological systems; designing and protecting systems that seek to dominate the battlespace.
- **Electronic warfare:** Uses radio-electronic and cryptographic techniques to degrade communication.
- **Psychological warfare:** Propaganda and terror to demoralize an adversary.
- **Hacker warfare:** Shutdown of systems, data errors, theft, and system monitoring using viruses, logic bombs, trojans, etc.
- **Economic warfare:** Blocking the flow of information to affect the economy of a business or nation.
- **Cyberwarfare:** Use of information systems against virtual personas (information terrorism, semantic attacks, simula-warfare).

**Defense vs Offense:**
- **Defensive Information Warfare:** Strategies and actions to defend against attacks on ICT assets.
- **Offensive Information Warfare:** Attacks against the ICT assets of an opponent.
