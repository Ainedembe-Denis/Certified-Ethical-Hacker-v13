## 1. Information Assurance (IA)

IA refers to assuring the integrity, availability, confidentiality, and authenticity of information systems during usage, processing, storage, and transmission. It utilizes physical, technical, and administrative controls.

**Key IA Processes:**
- Developing local policy, process, and guidance.
- Designing secure network and user authentication strategies.
- Performing vulnerability assessments.
- Performing Certification and Accreditation (C&A) processes.
- Providing information assurance training.

---

## 2. Continual/Adaptive Security Strategy

A strategy dictating continuous actions to ensure comprehensive defense.
- **Protect:** Prior countermeasures (policies, firewalls, IDS) to eliminate vulnerabilities.
- **Detect:** Assessing the network for abnormalities via continuous monitoring and packet sniffing.
- **Respond:** Identifying root causes, containing, and eradicating threats (Incident Response).
- **Predict:** Identifying potential future attacks through risk assessments and threat intelligence.

---

## 3. Defense-in-Depth

A military-inspired strategy using multiple, layered protection mechanisms. If an attacker breaches one layer, they are immediately faced with another, delaying the intrusion and giving administrators time to deploy countermeasures. (Layers: Policies -> Physical -> Perimeter -> Internal Network -> Host -> Application -> Data).

## 4. Risk Management

**Risk** is the degree of uncertainty or potential damage an adverse event may cause.
> **Formula:** RISK = Threat × Vulnerability × Asset Value
> **Formula:** Level of Risk = Consequence × Likelihood

**Risk Matrix Levels:**
- **Extreme/High:** Serious imminent danger (immediate measures required).
- **Medium:** Moderate danger (implement controls quickly).
- **Low:** Negligible danger (preventive steps).

**Risk Management Phases:**
1. **Risk Identification:** Identify sources, causes, and consequences of risks.
2. **Risk Assessment:** Estimate the likelihood and impact; assign priorities for mitigation.
3. **Risk Treatment:** Select and implement controls based on severity level.
4. **Risk Tracking and Review:** Continuously monitor and inspect policies to ensure effectiveness.

## 5. Cyber Threat Intelligence (CTI)

CTI involves collecting and analyzing information about threats and adversaries to make knowledgeable decisions for preparedness and prevention.

**Types of Threat Intelligence:**
- **Strategic:** High-level info on cyber posture and financial impacts consumed by executives (CISO).
- **Tactical:** Information on adversary TTPs used by security operations (NOC staff) to deploy technical mitigations.
- **Operational:** Contextual info on specific threats, helping Incident Response (IR) and forensic teams.
- **Technical:** Specific IoCs (malware hashes, C2 IPs) for rapid distribution to SOC teams and IPS/IDS devices.

**Threat Intelligence Lifecycle:**
1. **Planning and Direction:** Defining requirements and building the team.
2. **Collection:** Gathering data via HUMINT, OSINT, signals, etc.
3. **Processing and Exploitation:** Decrypting, parsing, and formatting raw data.
4. **Analysis and Production:** Analyzing facts and forecasts to elevate data into actionable intelligence.
5. **Dissemination and Integration:** Distributing reports to stakeholders and feeding data into security tools.

## 6. Threat Modeling

An approach for analyzing application security by understanding the adversary’s perspective and characterizing threats.

**Five Steps of Threat Modeling:**
1. **Identify Security Objectives:** Determine what data must be protected and compliance needs.
2. **Application Overview:** Diagram the end-to-end deployment, data flows, and trust boundaries.
3. **Decompose the Application:** Identify trust boundaries (where trust levels change), entry points, exit points, and data flows.
4. **Identify Threats:** Group common threats using a question-driven approach.
5. **Identify Vulnerabilities:** Identify weaknesses related to the discovered threats and fix them.

## 7. Incident Management

A defined process to identify, analyze, prioritize, and resolve security incidents to restore normal operations.

**Incident Handling and Response (IH&R) Steps:**
1. **Preparation:** Auditing assets, building/training the team, defining policies.
2. **Incident Recording and Assignment:** Initial reporting and assigning tickets.
3. **Incident Triage:** Analyzing, validating, and prioritizing the incident.
4. **Notification:** Informing stakeholders and management.
5. **Containment:** Preventing spread to other assets.
6. **Evidence Gathering and Forensic Analysis:** Investigating the root cause and attack method.
7. **Eradication:** Removing the root cause and closing attack vectors.
8. **Recovery:** Restoring systems without disrupting business.
9. **Post-Incident Activities:** Documenting, reviewing policies, and disclosure.

## 8. Role of AI and ML in Cyber Security

Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Machine Learning (ML) can process massive datasets to detect anomalies that traditional signatures miss.
- **Supervised Learning:** Uses labeled training data for classification and regression.
- **Unsupervised Learning:** Uses unlabeled data for clustering and dimensionality reduction.

**How AI/ML Prevent Cyber Attacks:**
- **Password Protection:** Improving biometric validations and face recognition.
- **Phishing Detection:** Scanning and identifying malicious emails faster than humans.
- **Threat Detection:** Deep learning forecasts imminent cyber threats.
- **Vulnerability Management:** Dynamically scanning and forecasting exploitation.
- **Behavioral Analytics:** Generating specific user patterns and alerting on deviations.
- **AI-based Antivirus:** Using anomaly detection rather than static signature matching.
- **Fraud & Botnet Detection:** Identifying payment inconsistencies and bypassing traditional IDS limitations.
