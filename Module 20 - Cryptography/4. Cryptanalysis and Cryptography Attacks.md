## 1. Cryptanalysis Methods

Cryptanalysis is the study of ciphers and cryptosystems to identify vulnerabilities and extract plaintext without knowing the key.

*   **Linear Cryptanalysis:** Discovers linear approximations (equations) describing the behavior of a block cipher (XORing plaintext and ciphertext bits to deduce the key). Invented by Mitsuru Matsui. Requires a large volume of known plaintexts.
*   **Differential Cryptanalysis:** Examines the differences in an input and how that affects the resultant output difference. Typically a chosen-plaintext attack applicable to symmetric algorithms.
*   **Integral Cryptanalysis:** Evaluates inputs that differ in only one bit position, keeping the rest constant. Useful against substitution-permutation block ciphers.
*   **Quantum Cryptanalysis:** Uses quantum computers (Shor's algorithm for factoring RSA/ECDH in polynomial time, Grover's algorithm for brute-forcing AES/SHA) to crack cryptography rapidly.

## 2. Code Breaking Methodologies

*   **Brute Force:** Exhaustive search trying every possible combination of characters. Time required doubles for every additional bit of key length. (e.g., A 56-bit key takes days; a 128-bit key takes 10^19 years).
*   **Frequency Analysis:** Studying the frequency of letters/words. If "k" appears most often in a ciphertext, it likely represents "e" in English. Source code is highly vulnerable due to repeated keywords (`#define`, `return`).
*   **Trickery and Deceit:** Using social engineering or phishing to trick users into handing over keys.
*   **One-Time Pad:** The only mathematically unbreakable cipher. Uses a random, non-repeating key that is exactly as long as the plaintext message. Used once and destroyed.

## 3. Cryptography Attacks

*   **Ciphertext-only Attack:** Attacker only has the ciphertext. Must look for patterns to deduce the key. Most difficult but most common scenario.
*   **Known-plaintext Attack:** Attacker has both the plaintext and the corresponding ciphertext. They use this to deduce the key used to encrypt other messages.
*   **Chosen-plaintext Attack:** Attacker chooses specific plaintexts to be encrypted and observes the resulting ciphertexts to derive the key.
*   **Chosen-ciphertext Attack:** Attacker selects arbitrary ciphertexts and tricks the receiver into decrypting them, observing the resulting plaintext to deduce the key. (Variants: Lunchtime/Midnight attack, Adaptive chosen-ciphertext attack).
*   **Related-Key Attack:** Attacker obtains ciphertexts encrypted under two different but mathematically related keys (e.g., WEP attacks leveraging predictable Initialization Vectors).
*   **Dictionary Attack:** Attacker pre-computes a dictionary of plaintexts and their hashes, then compares captured hashes against the dictionary.
*   **Rainbow Table Attack:** Uses a time-memory trade-off. Pre-computed tables of password hashes. Much faster than brute-force.
*   **Hash Collision Attack:** Finding two different inputs that produce the exact same hash output (common vulnerability in MD5 and SHA-1). Allows forging digital signatures.
*   **Birthday Attack:** A mathematical brute-force attack exploiting the birthday paradox (e.g., in a room of 23 people, there is a 50% chance two share a birthday). It drastically reduces the number of attempts needed to find a hash collision.
*   **Meet-in-the-Middle Attack:** Targets multiple encryption schemes (like Double DES). Encrypts from one end, decrypts from the other, and looks for a match in the middle, bypassing the mathematical strength of double encryption.
*   **Side-Channel Attack:** A physical attack observing environmental factors of the hardware doing the cryptography.
    *   *Power Consumption (SPA/DPA):* Observing spikes in power during specific operations.
    *   *Electromagnetic/Light Emission:* Reading radiation or LED status indicators.
    *   *Timing/Delay:* Measuring the exact time a CPU takes to process an encryption request.
    *   *Sound:* Acoustic emissions from CPUs or keyboards.
*   **DUHK Attack (Don't Use Hard-Coded Keys):** Affects devices using ANSI X9.31 PRNGs with hardcoded seed keys, allowing attackers to decrypt VPN traffic.
*   **DROWN Attack:** A cross-protocol attack. If a server supports obsolete SSLv2 alongside modern TLS, an attacker can launch SSLv2 probes to decrypt modern TLS traffic.
*   **Padding Oracle Attack:** Exploits block ciphers (CBC mode). The attacker manipulates ciphertext padding and observes the server's error responses ("Invalid Padding") to decipher the message byte-by-byte without the key.
*   **Rubber Hose Attack:** Extracting cryptographic secrets via physical coercion or torture of the key holder.

## 4. Blockchain Attacks

*   **51% Attack (Majority Attack):** Attackers gain control of more than 50% of the network's mining power. They mine a private chain, outpace the public chain, and broadcast it, allowing them to reverse transactions and double-spend.
*   **Finney Attack:** The attacker pre-mines a block containing a transaction to themselves. They then buy a real-world good with a zero-confirmation transaction. Immediately after receiving the good, they broadcast the pre-mined block, invalidating the payment to the vendor.
*   **Race Attack:** Exploits network latency. The attacker broadcasts a payment transaction to a vendor, and immediately broadcasts a conflicting transaction to the rest of the network, hoping the network confirms the second one first.
*   **Eclipse Attack:** Isolating a target node by filling its peer tables with malicious IPs. The target node's view of the blockchain is completely manipulated by the attacker.
*   **DeFi Sandwich Attack:** Targets Automated Market Makers (AMMs). The attacker sees a large pending buy order in the mempool, places their own buy order just before it (front-running), and places a sell order right after it, profiting from the artificial price inflation.

## 5. Cryptanalysis Tools

*   **CrypTool (CT1, CT2, JCT):** A comprehensive suite of software tools for visual programming, execution of cascades, and cryptanalysis of classical and modern algorithms (RSA, AES).
*   **VeraCrypt Brute-Forcing:** Attackers extract the volume header using `dd.exe` and pass it to `hashcat` to crack the volume password.
    *   `hashcat.exe -a 3 -w 1 -m 13721 <hashfile.tc> ?d?d?d?d`
*   **Other Tools:** RsaCtfTool, Msieve, Cryptol, CryptoSMT, MTP.
